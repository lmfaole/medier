## What this project is

`music` is a personal listening-history dashboard at sky.lmfaole.party/apps/medier/musikk. A
Rust tool (`sync-rust/`) fetches new listens from the ListenBrainz API on a
by hand, writes them to the local database (`.data/music.db`), and recomputes summary
tables (top artists/tracks/albums, a listening-hour heatmap, streaks) so the
Worker only ever does cheap, precomputed reads.

An **album** is not its exact release name: `music-sync variants` (`album_key.rs`, `song_key.rs`) gives every spelling in `listens` a key (artist and title with case, punctuation, a featured tail and edition tags like «Deluxe Edition» or «2011 Remaster» folded away; a remix or live version stays apart) and writes `album_variant`, `artist_variant` and `song_variant`; spellings with one key are one album, artist or song, called by its most played spelling. The view `canon_listens` (in `db/music.sql`) is `listens` under those names: the summaries, `entity_artists.rs` and `entity_songs.rs` read it (`FROM canon_listens AS listens`), so a spelling never makes a second entry, and `variants` runs before them. `entities` writes `entity_alias` (the address a joined spelling used to have), which the Worker answers with the page it joined and the route redirects to. Each track of an album page carries what is known of it (length, first and last played, loved, genres, a credit that is not the album's artist, a year that is not the album's: `album_tracks.rs`, `lib/track-line.ts`). **A collaboration counts for every artist on it** (`canon_artist_listens`, the view with one row per artist of a listen: the artist pages, the top artists and the unique-artist count read it; songs and albums keep `canon_listens`). Who is on a listen comes from MusicBrainz' credit of the recording (`music-sync recording-credits <limit>`, `credits.rs`, `recording_credit`; a group such as «Aly & AJ» is one credit), else from `artist_split` (`collab.rs`: a name is split only when every part is an artist heard alone), else the listened name stays one. An artist page lists all its albums and songs, who it was heard with (`collaborations`, with the tracks they share) and the artists the library files under alike genres (`similar`, `artist_relations.rs`). `.data/music-artists.csv` (optional, by hand; `alias_name,canonical_name`, `artist_alias.rs`) is for an artist that changed its name (Marina and the Diamonds is MARINA): the old name joins the new one in all three variant tables and the artist is called by the new name, not the most played; the old page address redirects through `entity_alias`. `.data/music-albums.csv` (optional, by hand; `artist,title,canonical_artist,canonical_title,release_mbid`) overrides the key: a line sends one spelling to the album it is, an empty canonical title keeps it apart, `release_mbid` pins the release whose tracklist is used. `music-sync album-tracklists <limit>` asks MusicBrainz for the tracklist of the most played albums (`album_tracklist`, `album_track`), landed under `.data/raw/musicbrainz/`; an album page then lists every track in the album's order (unplayed dimmed), played tracks not on it after, and an album without a tracklist keeps the played tracks, most played first.

What the owner **has or wants** is the collection: `.data/music-collection.csv`, written by hand (`added_on,kind,artist,title,year,status,rating,note,mbid,format`; `kind` is `artist`, `album` or `song`, an artist's `title` is empty, `status` is `owned` or `want`). `music-sync import-collection` makes `music_collection` exactly its rows. `format` is what the owner has (`cd`, `vinyl`, `digital`, `blu-ray`, `steam`, a platform; free text, lowercase, several with commas) and is shown as «Eier» on the work's page and in the library. In the library (`/bibliotek`) a line that is also played stays heard and is marked owned; one that is not played has a page of its own (`kind: 'collection'`, from the collection alone). Every page of a work tells the collection's lines (owned or wanted, form, the day added, rating; never the note). **Discogs** also keeps what it says besides the copy (`discogs_detail`: genres, styles and the master with its year, which replaces a reissue's own year; `discogs_cover`: the MusicBrainz release that names the Discogs address, else the best release group of the artist and title, and its Cover Art Archive front image, so a work only owned has a cover and a popover with genres, styles, label and catalogue number). A collection line and a played album are matched by letters and digits only, so «Janet.» and «Janet» are one. **Discogs** (`music-sync discogs`; `DISCOGS_USER`, `DISCOGS_KEY`, `DISCOGS_SECRET` in `music-sync.env`, the collection and wantlist public in Discogs' privacy settings) lands both lists under `.data/raw/discogs/<date>/`, makes `discogs_item` exactly them and lays them over `music_collection` (`note` is `discogs:<id>`; a CSV line for the same album wins; `import-collection` lays them over again).

The **concerts** the owner has been to are `.data/music-concerts.csv` (`watched_on,title,place`; `sync-rust/src/concerts.rs`, on `shared_data::watch_log` like the collection): `music-sync import-concerts` makes `music_concert` exactly its lines, and `music-sync add-concert <title> <venue> <date>` adds one. **Artists per concert:** a line is a day and a title, so a festival is one line a day (the same title, e.g. `Slottsfjell 2017`) and a night with a support act is one line; `.data/music-concert-acts.csv` (`watched_on,title,artist,billing`; `billing` is `headliner`, `support` or empty) says which artists the owner saw there, matched to a concert (the log's or the calendar's) by day and title. `import-concerts` makes `music_concert_act` exactly its lines (the file is optional); `music-sync add-concert-act <title> <date> <artist> <billing|->` adds one to a concert already in the log. **Albums on an artist's page:** the list shows every album, and only albums: a release `music-sync album-types <limit>` found a MusicBrainz release group for, by the artist's and the album's name (the album's name without an edition when the whole one finds none), typed `Album` and not a DJ mix, a demo, the spoken word, an interview or an audiobook (`album_type`; the recording's own release group, `release_group_info`, says too little, as it is the album a single is on). It asks the most played albums of the 300 most played artists that were heard in two tracks or more, one request a second, and lands each answer under `.data/raw/musicbrainz/`; an album with no such group has a row with no type and is not asked again (delete the row to ask again). Every other release the artist was heard on is «Andre» in the years chart, which divides its columns by all of the albums. An album that is not typed is not on the list until the job has asked, so run it before `entities` after new plays. **Chart colours:** the charts of an artist, an album and a song take their colours from the album covers: `npm run cover:colors` reads each cover under `web/public/covers/` into one colour (the hue most of its colourful pixels share, set to a lightness that reads on both themes) in `.data/music-cover-colors.csv`, and `music-sync import-cover-colors` makes `music_cover_color` of it. An artist's years chart divides each column by album in the album's colour (`albumColors`, `YearsChart`; the grey steps stay for an album with no colour and for «Andre»), and a chart of one series uses the page's `accent` (the cover's colour; the artist's is its most played album's). The charts take them as `colors` and `color` (`@lmfaole/designsystem` 3.1.0). **Album names:** an album is named as the list names its concert, «Konsert: artist, venue, year», and a festival has one album for all its days, «Festival: name and year» (`concertAlbumName`, `lib/concert-label.ts`); `npm run concert:albums [-- --apply]` renames the albums of the concerts to match and merges a festival's day albums into one (deleting the emptied ones, never a photo), and `event-albums` finds an album by the id it wrote down, so a rename does not make a second album. **Albums for a log-only concert:** `npm run concert:album -- <date> <title> [from] [to] --apply` makes the Immich album `Konsert: <title> (<date>)` from the photos of the day (by local time, whole day by default), writes it in `.data/music-concert-albums.csv` (`watched_on,title,album_id`), and imports it (`music_concert_album`, matched by day and title); then `music-sync concert-photos` keeps its favourites. A concert's title is «artist, venue» in the list and on its page; a **festival** (artists written for it, none billed `headliner` or `support`) is just its title, name and year. `music-sync setlist-lineup <date> <city>` (`SETLISTFM_API_KEY` in `music-sync.env`) lists the artists setlist.fm has for a day in a city and keeps the answer under `.data/raw/setlistfm/`; it writes nothing to the log, the owner says which were seen. **Support acts:** an act's `billing` is `support`, a headliner `headliner`; a concert with a headliner is titled for the headliners only and its page lists the support acts as such. `music-sync setlist-support <title> <date> [--add]` lists the other artists setlist.fm has at the concert's venue on its day (support, or another stage of the same venue: the owner decides) and `--add` writes them as support, with the artist the title names as headliner. `music-sync setlist-songs` keeps the setlists setlist.fm has of the concerts that have been (`music_concert_setlist`, a row a song; one for each artist written for the concert, else for the artist its title names; the answers under `.data/raw/setlistfm/setlists/`), and a concert's page shows them right after its head, linking to setlist.fm as its terms ask. The acts show on `/konserter` and on `/konsert/<slug>`, each a link when the artist has a page. `npm run mail:concerts` writes them from the Ticketmaster receipts that `mail-ingest` landed (read by their schema.org reservation, never their sender), and puts each in the Nextcloud calendar *Konserter* (`concert-ticket.ts` of the Nextcloud repository; `npm run concert:move` there moved the old ones out of *Sosialt*). `music-sync import-concerts-calendar` (`concert_calendar.rs`) makes `music_concert_event` exactly that calendar's events (upcoming and past), each with its Immich album id from `raw/immich/event-albums.json` of the Nextcloud repository (written by `event-albums`; only the id is kept, the link is built in `lib/immich.ts`). `server/concerts.ts` joins them with the older log lines, and the pages are `/konserter`, `/konserter/venuer` (a subpage, linked from `/konserter`; the old `/venuer` is a 301) and `/venue/<slug>`, and each concert has its own page, `/konsert/<slug>` (`features/concerts/concert-body.tsx`). **Decided 2026-11-11: the concert pages are shown, behind the login like the rest of the app.** `music-sync concert-photos` keeps the Immich photos and videos of each concert album (eighteen pictures, a video as its still, in table `music_concert_media`: the album's cover first, which the owner sets in Immich (*Use as album cover*) and which is the head of the concert's page, then the favourites, then others spread over the time after the start) as small pictures in `.data/concert-photos/`, which the music server serves itself (`server/concert-photo.ts`, mounted in `scripts/media-server.ts`) so a page never asks Immich; they are photos of people and places, shown only behind the login. A venue is a place, so the concerts and their photos have no file in a snapshot (`snapshot.test.ts` fails if one gets it).

**Notifications** at the end of a week (Sunday 20:00) and a month (last day, 20:00): two switches on `/tilbakeblikk` (`views/PushToggles.tsx`, `lib/push-client.ts`) subscribe the browser to Web Push; what is on is the browser's own (its push subscription and `localStorage`), not read from the server. The server keeps only the address and keys a push needs, in `.data/push/subscriptions.json` (`server/push.ts`, the media server's one write, through the `write` route of `scripts/media-router.ts`). `scripts/music-digest.ts` (`keys` once: VAPID keys in `~/.config/music-push.json`, copied to `secrets/` by `npm run machine -- pull`, the public one in `.data/push/`; `install` the two timers; `send week|month`; `test`) builds the text from `weeks()` or `months()` (`lib/digest.ts`: the ending week or month compared with the one before as if over; nothing is sent when nothing was heard), sends it with `web-push` and forgets a device the push service says is gone. The service worker (`packages/config/pwa/sw.ts`) shows it and a tap opens the page. A phone needs the app installed (on iPhone, on the home screen).

## Commands

```sh
npm test                 # node --test: the Worker's wiring, SQL queries and web/ formatting
npm run typecheck        # page and Worker, one TypeScript project
npm run dev              # `astro dev`: the page and the real Worker, serving the snapshot (run `npm run snapshot` first)
npm run dev:worker       # production build, then `astro preview` in workerd
npm run build            # web/dist/client (page) and web/dist/server (Worker)
npm run sync -- <job>    # one sync job, e.g. `summaries current-year` (needs cargo; writes .data/music.db, or MUSIC_DB; jobs in sync-rust/src/main.rs)
npm run snapshot         # the API as static files in web/public/api/, from .data/music.db
npm run deploy           # build and `wrangler deploy`
```

music runs on Astro with React islands, rendering on the server (`docs/UI-COMPONENTS.md` at the
repository root): one Astro project builds the page and the Worker, and `astro dev` runs the Worker in workerd, which serves `/api/*` from the snapshot in
`web/public/api/` exactly as production does, so there is no proxy and no separate
`wrangler dev`. Run `npm run snapshot` first, or `/api/*` answers 404.

`npm run sync` needs `LASTFM_USER`, `LASTFM_API_KEY` (new scrobbles, the daily source) in the environment (`LISTENBRAINZ_USERNAME` only for the old `listens` job) and nothing
from Cloudflare; it runs by hand (the scheduled workflow is gone). The deployed
Worker serves a snapshot with one file per period and limit the page asks for
(`web/src/server/snapshot.ts`), so run `npm run deploy` after a sync to publish
it (`docs/DATA.md`).

## Specific to this app

- `web/src/server/api.ts` only reads the database, at snapshot time. `sync-rust/` only writes to it.
  The Worker reads precomputed tables, with one exception: `/api/recent.json`
  reads the last 5, 10 or 15 rows of `listens` itself, through `idx_listens_time`,
  because "the last listen" cannot be precomputed.
  and `web/src/server/cover-art.ts` fetches it from the Cover Art Archive once per
  Cloudflare data centre, then serves it from the edge cache, immutable for
  a year in the browser. `img-src` therefore stays `'self'`; do not link
  an image host directly.
- `summary_periods`, `top_items`, `top_item_covers`,
  `daily_plays`, `day_memories`, `month_capsules`, `entity_pages`, `breakdowns` and `metadata_coverage` are derived and safe to delete; `listens` and
  `sync_state` are not. `recording_metadata`/`recording_genres` are a cache
  that takes several rate-limited syncs to refill.
- A new derived table is written through `diff::sync_table`, not delete-and-insert, so
  an unchanged table writes nothing; the older tables (`top_items`, `breakdowns` …) still are delete-and-insert.
- `db/music.sql` is applied by every sync, so every statement in it must
  stay idempotent; `schema.rs` has a test that enforces it.
- Never commit a ListenBrainz data dump.
- Before saying done, also run
  `cargo test --manifest-path sync-rust/Cargo.toml` when Rust is touched.
  The aggregation tests run the real SQL against SQLite.
- Architecture and data flow: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`. Deployment, secrets
  and the sync schedule: `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`.
- **Component tests are not run** if they are `.tsx`: `node --test` strips types from `.ts` only,
  so logic that needs a test lives in `lib/*.ts` (`format.ts`).

## Podcasts are not music

Podcast plays are moved out of `listens` into `.data/podcasts.db` by `split-podcasts` (second job of the daily and weekly timers). After a manual `import-lastfm`, `import-spotify` or `listens`, run `split-podcasts` before the recompute chain. The rest (new shows, feeds, countries, `scope.rs` and `MUSIC_ONLY_SQL`) is `docs/PODCASTS.md`.
